From the redesigned document

What we do now will determine whether entire cultures survive or disappear. Climate change is not a distant threat—it has already broken the foundations of daily life for millions, from sinking island nations to scorched farmlands. As seas rise and soils dry, we look away, forgetting that geography is not morality: a Tuvaluan fisherman and a student in California breathe the same air, drink the same water, and rely on the same planet. Yet our political and economic systems treat some lives as disposable, framing climate migration as a humanitarian inevitability rather than confronting the structures that created it. Should climate migration be treated as an unavoidable crisis of displaced people, or as evidence of global systems of inequality that demand corporate accountability?

Climate migration is not an unpredictable tragedy but a manufactured outcome of fossil- fuel corporations knowingly destabilizing the water, food, and land systems of vulnerable nations. The displacement of Tuvaluans, Guatemalan farmers, and Central American migrants shows that climate collapse is designed—not natural—and the responsibility for repair must fall on the companies that created the crisis, not on the communities forced to abandon their homelands. This paper first examines how climate collapse destroys basic conditions of life, making migration a survival response rather than a choice. It then traces how fossil-fuel corporations produced this crisis through emissions, disinformation, and political obstruction. Finally, it argues that relocation is cultural erasure and that true climate accountability requires mitigation and reparations instead of forcing displaced people to assimilate elsewhere.
